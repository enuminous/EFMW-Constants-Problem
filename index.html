The Constants Problem

Physics already knows that its fundamental constants form a network. The harder problem is to determine which quantities are independent inputs, which are derived identities, which depend on unit conventions, and whether a deeper theory can predict a dimensionless constant without fitting it.

From “all constants are connected” to a real scientific question

The 2025 paper One Paper to Join Them All was strongest where it described the established network connecting gravitation, electromagnetism, quantum mechanics and relativity. But relatedness is not derivation.

α = e² / (4π ε₀ ħ c)
c⁻² = μ₀ ε₀
ℓP² = ħG/c³   ·   tP² = ħG/c⁵   ·   mP² = ħc/G

These equations prove that constants participate in common structures. They do not prove that one deeper law generates their measured values.

The distinction that matters

ClassMeaning
IdentityAn exact algebraic relationship inside an established theory.
InputA quantity whose value must be measured or stipulated.
ConventionA numerical value partly fixed by our unit system.
PredictionA quantity derived from fewer independently specified assumptions.

The deepest target is a dimensionless quantity. Dimensionful numbers change when units change. Dimensionless constants do not.

Therefore the serious EFMW target is not “derive c in meters per second.” It is something like: derive α from independently fixed FieldSpace invariants, symmetry data, topology, or normalized couplings—with no fitted α hiding on the right-hand side.

The modern EFMW hypothesis

𝒢(Θ) → {dimensionless couplings, invariant ratios, effective constants}

Here Θ must be a smaller primitive structure than the output set. If α is already encoded in Θ, reproducing α is circular.

What the Lean formalization proves

The included Lean project machine-checks algebraic consequences of the standard fine-structure, Maxwell, and Planck-scale relations. It also defines an abstract generator interface and an alpha-free integer-ratio toy example. That toy is not a proof of non-circularity: its integers could still be selected after seeing the target.

Formal result: the logical distinction between standard identities and a candidate generator is now explicit and machine-readable. Not a result: the measured value of α has not been derived from EFMW.

The research program

A serious EFMW constants program should now:

  1. enumerate every primitive FieldSpace coefficient;
  2. separate dimensionful from dimensionless parameters;
  3. remove unit-convention redundancy;
  4. identify symmetry and reciprocity reductions;
  5. count the truly independent dimensionless inputs;
  6. attempt to derive a measured dimensionless coupling from the remainder.

Conclusion

The constants already belong to one physical network. A unifying theory earns something new only when it reduces what must be assumed.

That is the corrected meaning of “one paper to join them all.” Not a page where every constant appears—but a theory in which at least one formerly independent number becomes unavoidable.
